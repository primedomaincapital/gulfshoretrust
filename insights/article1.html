The Great Wealth Migration to Florida's Gulf Coast

Tax policy, demographics, and the quiet reordering of American private wealth.
Prime Domain Capital · October 2026 · 6 min read

Every year, the Internal Revenue Service publishes migration data that reads like a slow-motion earthquake. For more than a decade, the direction has been unmistakable: adjusted gross income is leaving high-tax states — New York, New Jersey, Illinois, California — and arriving in Florida at a pace with no modern precedent. Between 2020 and 2023 alone, Florida gained tens of billions in net AGI from domestic migration, more than any other state by a wide margin.

But the headline numbers obscure the geography that matters. The wealth isn't dispersing evenly across the peninsula. It is concentrating — with remarkable precision — along the Gulf Coast corridor from Sarasota to Naples.

Why the Gulf, specifically

Southwest Florida offers a combination that exists almost nowhere else in the country: no state income tax, no estate tax, a homestead exemption that caps annual assessment increases, and a coastline that has been, for three generations, the winter address of American industrial wealth. The infrastructure of affluence — private clubs, marinas, philanthropic institutions, top-tier medical centers — was built decades ago. New arrivals aren't pioneering; they're joining.

Collier County, home to Naples, routinely ranks among the highest per-capita income counties in the nation. The median home price in Port Royal, Naples' most storied neighborhood, sits in the eight figures. This is not a market of aspirational wealth. It is a market of arrived wealth, much of it in the second or third generation of stewardship.

“The families arriving on the Gulf Coast aren't asking whether to establish trusts. They're asking which trustee deserves the responsibility.”

What this means for fiduciaries

Wealth in motion creates demand for trust services at both ends of the journey. Families relocating to Florida frequently restructure existing trusts to take advantage of the state's favorable regime — including its 1,000-year rule against perpetuities, among the most permissive in the country. Each restructuring is a trustee-selection event. Each selection event is won, overwhelmingly, by firms with local presence and recognizable names.

The trust companies winning this business share a trait: they sound like they belong. A national brand with a Florida office competes against a Gulf Coast name that needs no introduction. In a relationship business where the first meeting often determines the next thirty years, the name on the door is not decoration. It is strategy.

The durable trend

Demographic projections suggest the migration has room to run. The baby-boom generation — the wealthiest cohort in American history — is still in the early stages of its retirement relocation wave. Remote work has untethered a younger tier of high earners from office geography. And state tax differentials, the original accelerant, show no sign of narrowing.

For fiduciaries positioned on the Gulf Shore, the question is not whether the demand will arrive. It is whether the name above the door will be the one clients remember.
